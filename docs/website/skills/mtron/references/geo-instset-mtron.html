    • geo instruction set

    The GeoJSON (RFC 7946) model at /m/math/geo, in six types: a point IS a position, a line IS an ordered list of points, a polygon IS a list of lines — the kind is the type's name, and there is no type or coordinates key anywhere in the model. Every Multi* form is a coefficient, a Feature is a rec with a geo:-prefixed fiber, and the JSON vocabulary lives only in ObjGeoJSONSerializer. Plus the geodesic and planar measures (distance, length, area), the containment filter (has/inside), the shape reductions as casts (as(point::T) is the centroid, as(bbox2d::T) the envelope), and the two encodings (GeoJSON and WKT). TRIGGER: when reading or writing a GeoJSON document, building a geometry from a list or from text, measuring a geometry, asking whether one geometry contains, lies inside or intersects another, handing a geometry to a spatial database as WKT, or declaring a native spatial pushdown.
    geo-instset-mtron.md

    the geographic instruction set (/m/math/geo)

    /m/math/geo is GeoJSON with the JSON taken out of it. The wire format's type, coordinates, geometry, properties and features keys exist because JSON has no type system: a point cannot say "I am a position", so the document says it for the point. mtron does not need the help. A point IS a position. A line IS an ordered list of points. A polygon IS a list of lines. The model is the value, the kind is the value's type name, and everything GeoJSON adds on top — the Multi* synonyms, the feature wrapper, the properties bag — is a mapping that happens at the boundary and nowhere else.

    The names carry no geo_ prefix: the instruction set's namespace IS the prefix.

    mtron> import(/m/math/geo,geo)
...

    the model

    lst::T@geometry                                         any list: a shape on the earth
geometry::T[?[real::T,real::T,real{?}::T]]@point        a coordinate pair, or a triple with an altitude
geometry::T[?[point{+}::T]]@line                        an ORDERED list of points
geometry::T[?[line{+}::T]]@polygon                      a shell line, then zero or more hole lines

lst::T@bbox                                             the nominal base of an envelope
bbox::T[?[real::T,real::T,real::T,real::T]]@bbox2d      [λmin, φmin, λmax, φmax]
bbox::T[?[real::T,real::T,real::T,real::T,real::T,real::T]]@bbox3d   [λmin, φmin, hmin, λmax, φmax, hmax]

    Six types. The two bases declare no poly — a refinement declares its own shape, so geometry is the family label and point, line, polygon are the shapes:

    lst
├── geo:geometry          "a shape on the earth" — the instruction domain
│   ├── geo:point         [λ, φ[, h]]
│   ├── geo:line          [point{+}]
│   └── geo:polygon       [line{+}]
└── geo:bbox
    ├── geo:bbox2d        [λmin, φmin, λmax, φmax]
    └── geo:bbox3d        [λmin, φmin, hmin, λmax, φmax, hmax]

    There is no position, no multipoint, no multilinestring, no multipolygon, no geometrycollection and no feature type. A multi-point is a bag of points (point{2,}::T), a multi-line is a bag of lines, a collection is a bag of geometries, and a feature is a rec — coefficients and fields, not types. The bag/list distinction is real (a bag has no order, a line does) and JSON has no way to say it; that difference is the serializer's business.

    A point is two or more reals: the RFC's two or three, plus the further elements §3.1.1 allows after the altitude. A line needs at least one point and a polygon at least one line, and construction is checked against exactly those declarations:

    mtron> geo:point::[-123.13,44.47]                          [-- a point: a coordinate pair --]
==>geo:point::[-123.13,44.47]

    what the wire adds, and where

    The mapping table is the whole of GeoJSON's extra vocabulary, and every row is a serializer rule rather than a type:

    the modelthe document
    point::[λ,φ]{"type":"Point","coordinates":[λ,φ]}
    line::[[λ,φ],…]{"type":"LineString","coordinates":[[λ,φ],…]}
    polygon::[[[λ,φ],…],…]{"type":"Polygon","coordinates":[[[λ,φ],…],…]}
    a bag of points (point{2,}::T){"type":"MultiPoint","coordinates":[…]}
    a bag of lines{"type":"MultiLineString",…}
    a bag of polygons{"type":"MultiPolygon",…}
    a bag of mixed geometries{"type":"GeometryCollection","geometries":[…]}
    a rec with a geo:-prefixed fiber (rec[geo:+]::T){"type":"Feature","geometry":{…},"properties":{…}}
    a bag of such recs{"type":"FeatureCollection","features":[…]}
    bbox2d / bbox3dthe "bbox" field on an object

    The document's type is consumed on the way in (the tid says the kind) and derived on the way out; its coordinates is the value itself; a Feature's properties come back into the rec, flattened, because properties are just the rec's other fields. ObjGeoJSONSerializer is the only place any of these words appear.

    mtron> geo:point::[-123.13,44.47].as(json::T)      [-- json::'{"type":"Point","coordinates":[-123.13,44.47]}' --]
==>
   json::'{"type":"Point","coordinates":[-123.13,44.47]}'
   
   
mtron> geo:line::[[-123.13,44.47],[-121.18,45.61]].as(json::T)
==>
   json::'{"type":"LineString","coordinates":[[-123.13,44.47],[-121.18,45.61]]}'
   
   
mtron> '{"type":"Point","coordinates":[-123.13,44.47]}'.as(geo:point::T)   [-- back to the bare point --]
==>point::[-123.13,44.47]

    a feature is a rec, and the wild-fiber is how you say so

    Declare the fiber with the geo: namespace and the rec becomes a feature: everything else on the rec is its properties, and the geometry answers the geo instructions untyped — the engine offers the call to the values the fiber exposes, so the point inside the person receives it.

    mtron> person -> rec[geo:location]::T[?[name=>str::T]]@person
...
mtron> person::[name=>'marko',geo:location=>geo:point::[-123.0,44.5]]@marko
==>person::[name=>'marko',
            geo:location=>geo:point::[-123.0,44.5]]@marko
mtron> *marko.type()                                         [-- the point type: the geometry at the fiber answers --]
mtron> *marko.inside(geo:bbox2d::[-123.2,44.4,-122.9,44.6])  [-- the point itself, since the filter answers the contained --]
==>geo:point::[-123.0,44.5]
mtron> *marko>>geo:location                                   [-- the point itself --]
==>geo:point::[-123.0,44.5]

    On the way out that rec is a GeoJSON Feature — geometry is the fiber's value, properties is {name: "marko"} — and on the way back in the properties are flattened into the rec again. No feature type exists at either end.

    the questions

    instructionanswer
    g.type()the kind — the type's name, point::T, line::T, polygon::T
    geo:coordinates()every stored position, in document order, as stamped points
    geo:distance(g)geodesic metres to g; 0 when they intersect (a point in a polygon)
    geo:length()geodesic metres of every polyline and polygon boundary
    geo:area()square metres of the polygonal part, shell positive and holes negative
    has(g) / geo:inside(g)the containment FILTER: has answers the container, inside the contained; both take a geometry or an envelope
    geo:wkt() / as(json::T) / as(<kind>::T)the two string encodings, out and in

    The two shape reductions are casts, not instructions, because construction and conversion are the same door:

    castwhat it is
    plane::[…].as(point::T)the centroid — a point from any geometry
    line::[…].as(bbox2d::T)the envelope — the box around every stored position
    bbox3d::[…].as(bbox2d::T)the 2D box: z dropped, lossy and stated

    what the numbers mean

    • distance is the great-circle distance (haversine, IUGG mean radius 6371008.8 m) — exact on the sphere to centimetres, and 0 when the geometries intersect, so a point inside a polygon is at distance zero rather than at the distance to the nearest vertex;
    • length sums the same distance along each polyline, and along a polygon's boundary (closing the ring);
    • area is the planar shoelace formula scaled by \(R^2\cos\bar\varphi\) at the mean latitude of the stored positions: exact for a rectangle whose sides run along parallels and meridians, an approximation otherwise;
    • has/geo:inside are exact for a point, a vertex-on-vertex test plus a ray cast otherwise — one predicate, two subjects (the container, or the contained one, which is what a fibered rec preserves);
    • the envelope is always the axis-aligned box around every stored position.

    What is not modeled is stated rather than half-done: there is no antimeridian cutting (RFC 7946 §3.1.9), no ring closure check (a "ring" is a line, and the RFC's first-point-equals-last is the caller's to honour), no altitude in any measurement, and no 3D envelope on a geometry (a bbox3d is a value of its own, not a field the model carries).

    the algebra the rewriter may use

    geoInstSetLawTable declares what a rewrite may do to a geo instruction: a native spatial operator may be substituted only where the laws license it, so an undeclared instruction is one the optimizer must leave alone.

    instructionlawswhat it licenses
    geo:distancecommutative, pureoperand order is free, so two callers reach one native operator
    has / geo:insidepure, each other's INVERSEa space with ST_Within alone answers ST_Contains by swapping the operands
    geo:coordinates, geo:wktpurestructural projections have no effect, so a rewrite can move or drop them
    geo:area, geo:lengthpure, blockedmeasured here, not floatable to a native operator

    A geometry is not a ring, a monoid or a lattice: there is no plus of two geometries, so the family declares no carrier algebra and typeLawsUncached answers the empty rec. The measures are homomorphisms to the reals, not operations on geometries.

    pushdown

    The instructions are for spaces that have no spatial support. One that does — PostGIS behind /m/db/tble, a document store's geo operator, a graph database's predicate — should answer from(<path>).geo:inside(<polygon>) with an indexed operator and never materialize the geometry in the VM. geoRewrite provides the pushdown factories; nothing is registered by default, because a pushdown is a claim about a backend, not about this family.
